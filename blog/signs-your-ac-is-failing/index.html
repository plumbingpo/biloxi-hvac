Air Conditioner Warning Signs in Biloxi, MS

Home / Blog / Air Conditioner Warning Signs in Biloxi, MS

As the warmest months approach here in Biloxi, MS, our air conditioners become hard-working systems of our home comfort. These units operate constantly to keep our indoor spaces cool and comfortable. But like any mechanical system, air conditioners have a lifespan and will eventually begin to show signs of wear and tear, indicating that a costly failure might be on the horizon. Being aware of these symptoms can save you from a hot and inconvenient situation on the hottest day of the year, and potentially avoid more extensive problems in the long run. Here at Biloxi HVAC, we want to help you understand what to look out for.

Reduced Cooling Performance: Is Your Home Not as Cool as It Used to Be?

One of the most common and immediate signs that your air conditioner is struggling is a drop in its cooling capability. You could be setting the thermostat to colder temperatures only to still feel warm or humid indoors. Perhaps certain rooms are much warmer than others. This isn't just about comfort; it indicates a problem within the system. This could stem from various underlying issues. It could be a depleted refrigerant charge, which means your system isn't able to absorb and release heat effectively. Refrigerant is the lifeblood of your AC. Another common culprit is a dirty air filter, which restricts airflow and makes the unit work harder without achieving the desired cooling. If the evaporator coils are fouled, cooling suffers, as they cannot efficiently transfer heat. The compressor itself, the engine of your AC, could be giving out. If you're experiencing inadequate cooling, it's time to have a professional from Biloxi HVAC diagnose the problem.

Strange Noises: When Your AC Sounds Off

Your air conditioner should operate with a relatively consistent, low hum. If you start hearing unusual or loud noises coming from your unit, it's a definite sign that something is amiss. A grinding or high-pitched squeal frequently suggests problems with worn motor bearings. These bearings, critical for smooth operation, can wear down over time, leading to friction and eventual motor failure if not addressed. Electrical issues, like a failing relay, can manifest as constant clicking. Loud banging or clunking is especially worrisome, as they might suggest a loose part hitting another component. Don't delay in addressing these auditory warnings. Heeding these sound warnings can prevent minor issues from becoming major expenses.

Increased Energy Bills: Why Is My Electric Bill So High?

A subtle but significant sign of a struggling AC is a gradual rise in your electricity costs. When your energy consumption hasn't changed but your bills are spiking, your AC unit might be working harder than it should to achieve the same level of cooling. This inefficiency can be caused by a variety of factors. As mentioned, a blocked filter reduces airflow, forcing the fan motor to work overtime. Leaky ducts can allow cooled air to escape into unconditioned spaces like your attic or crawl space, meaning your AC has to run longer to maintain the desired temperature. An old or undersized unit might just be inefficient by design. Low refrigerant levels increase the workload on your AC. The compressor and fan motors can become less efficient as they age. If your utility costs are climbing for no apparent reason, it's worth having Biloxi HVAC inspect your AC unit to identify the source of the inefficiency. Our team can help you determine the most cost-effective solution.

Poor Airflow: Is Air Barely Coming Out of the Vents?

Strong airflow is essential for both comfort and system efficiency. If you notice that the air coming out of your vents is weak, or if some rooms have significantly less airflow than others, it's a clear warning of an underlying problem. This problem can stem from various causes. The most frequent cause is a heavily soiled or obstructed air filter, which restricts the volume of air that can pass through your system. If return vents are blocked, airflow will suffer. The fan motor, if failing, is another source of poor airflow. A struggling fan motor won't adequately circulate air through your home's ducts. Debris or even physical damage within the ductwork can also restrict air movement. Closed dampers in your duct system can lead to inconsistent airflow distribution. It's critical to resolve poor airflow to improve comfort and protect your AC from premature wear.

Excessive Moisture or Leaks: Unwanted Wetness Near Your System?

A little condensation is fine, but if you see puddles or a lot of wetness, there's an issue. This is a definite warning sign that something is wrong. Usually, water leaks are due to an obstructed condensate line. The AC dehumidifies, sending collected water through a condensate line. A clogged condensate line will cause water to back up and spill. This can result in damage to your property and even pose electrical risks. Beyond a clogged drain, a cracked or rusted drain pan can also cause leaks. If there's a major refrigerant leak, ice can build up on the coils, melt, and cause overflow. Any standing water or excessive moisture near your AC, it's imperative to address it quickly to prevent further damage. Biloxi HVAC can diagnose the source of the leak and provide a lasting solution.

Frequent Cycling: Is Your AC Turning On and Off Too Often?

Normal AC operation involves consistent cycles of cooling and then resting. If you notice your AC unit turning on and off much more frequently than usual, this is known as "short cycling" and it's a significant warning sign. In short cycling, the AC doesn't complete its cooling task, causing inefficiency and stress. There are various potential causes for this behavior. An oversized unit might short cycle because it cools the air too rapidly, then stops before it can remove humidity effectively. Often, short cycling is due to low refrigerant, a dirty filter, or a faulty thermostat. A failing compressor or an electrical issue can also lead to short cycling. Short cycling, regardless of its origin, quickly degrades AC parts, leading to early failure and higher bills. An AC that short cycles isn't working properly and requires professional diagnosis.

Old Age: The Inevitable March of Time for Your AC

The age of your AC unit, even if it seems fine, is a major predictor of future issues. Typically, central AC units last 10-15 years, with exceptional cases reaching 20 with good maintenance. Once your unit hits or passes this age, it becomes more susceptible to failure and loses efficiency. Older units often use outdated refrigerants like R-22, which are no longer manufactured and are becoming increasingly expensive to service due to supply limitations. Components in older systems are naturally more worn from years of use. Though maintenance helps, eventually repairs become too frequent and expensive compared to replacing with a new unit. If your AC is over a decade old and showing symptoms, it's wise to consider replacement options. A new, energy-efficient model can offer significant savings on your utility bills and provide superior comfort for years to come.
